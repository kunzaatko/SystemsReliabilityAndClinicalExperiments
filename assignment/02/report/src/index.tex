\section{Parametric Estimates}%
\label{sec:parametric_estimates}

Based on the histograms in \Cref{fig:histograms}, we will first attempt to fit an exponential distribution to the lifetime data.

\begin{theorem}[Likelihood functions for the \ac{RC} model]
    \label{thm:likelihood}
    Let \(F_T=F_T(t,\bs{\theta}_1)\) and \(F_C = F_C(c, \bs{\theta}_2)\) be distributions of \(T\) and \(C\)
    respectively, such that \(F_T\) and \(F_C\) do not contain the same parameters (even in a relationship sense). Let
    us have \((W_j, \delta_j)_1^n\) \ac{iid} \(F_{W,\delta}\) replications. Then
    \[
        L(\bs{\theta}_1, \bs{\theta}_2) = f_{\bs{W}, \bs{\delta}}(\bs{w}, \bs{\delta}; \bs{\theta}_1, \bs{\theta}_2) =
        L_1(\bs{\theta}_1) \cdot L_2(\bs{\theta}_2),
    \] where \[
        \begin{aligned}
            L_1(\bs{\theta}_1) &= \prod_{j\in D} f_T(w_j) \prod_{j \in V} (1 - F_T(w_j)) \\
            L_2(\bs{\theta}_2) &= \prod_{j\in D} (1-F_C(w_j)) \prod_{j \in V} f_C(w_j),
        \end{aligned}
    \] with the notation \(D = \left\{ j \mid \delta_j = 1 \right\}\) and \(V = \left\{ j\mid \delta_j = 0 \right\}\).
\end{theorem}

Using \Cref{thm:likelihood}, we can make a \ac{MLE} of the distribution \(F_T\) without assuming a distribution
of \(C\). Specifically for a \ac{MLE} of an exponentially distributed \(F_T\), we have \[
    L_1(\lambda) = \prod_{j\in D} f_T(w_j) \cdot \prod_{j \in V} R_T(w_j) = \prod_{j\in D} \lambda e^{-\lambda w_j}
    \cdot \prod_{j \in V} e^{-\lambda w_j} = \lambda^{|D|} \cdot \exp(-\lambda \sum_{j = 1}^n w_j),
\] and the \ac{LEs} and \ac{MLE} estimate follows to be
\begin{gather*}
    \ell_1(\lambda) = \ln L_1(\lambda) = |D| \cdot \ln \lambda - \lambda \sum_{j = 1}^n w_j \\
    \partial_\lambda\ell_1(\lambda) = \frac{|D|}{\lambda} - \sum_{j = 1}^n w_j = 0 \implies \hat{\lambda}_{\text{ML}}
        = \frac{|D|}{\sum_{j = 1}^n w_j}.
\end{gather*}

Checking the fit via a histogram while keeping the information about the data censoring is done via the \ac{KM}
estimate of the \ac{CDF}. The \ac{KM} model for censored data gives us the estimate for the survival function as
\[
    \widehat{S}(t)=\prod_{i:t_{i}\leq t}\left(1-{\frac{d_{i}}{n_{i}}}\right).
\] 

Therefore an estimate of the \ac{CDF} that takes the censors into account is 
\[
    \widehat{F}(t)=1-\widehat{S}(t).
\]

We can then create a Q-Q plot that accounts for the censors simply by evaluating the quantile at the
estimated quantile from the \ac{KM} model for each time and plotting against the true observed time. This equates to the
plot being \[
    \left\{ F^{-1}_T(\widehat{F}(t)), t  \right\} \qcomma  \forall t \in (t_i),
\]  where \(F_T\) is the distribution of that we want to check.

A P-P plot accounting for the censors is analogously \[
    \left\{ F_T(t), \widehat{F}(t) \right\} \qcomma  \forall t \in (t_i).
\]

Lets also try the Weibull distribution.

Similarly to the exponential distribution, we determine for the Weibull distribution
\begin{gather*}
    R_T(t) = \exp{-(\lambda t)^\alpha} = \left\{ \lambda^\alpha \eqqcolon \lambda' \right\} = \exp{-\lambda't^\alpha} \\
    f_T(t) = \lambda' \alpha t^{(\alpha - 1)} \exp{-\lambda't^\alpha}; \quad \lambda' > 0,\, \alpha > 0,\, t > 0
\end{gather*} the log-likelihood as
\[
    \ell_1(\alpha, \lambda') = |D| \ln \lambda' + |D| \ln \alpha + (\alpha - 1) \ln\prod_{j \in D} w_j - \lambda'
    \sum_{j = 1}^n w_j^\alpha.

\section{Summary}%
\label{sec:summary}
The main insight is given in the \Cref{fig:KM_AB}, where we can see that the survival function estimate is lower for
group \(A\) in the interval \((100, 400)\) days. I would say that the right tail of both groups does not include enough
observations for the estimate have a big weight in the conclusions. This indicates that if a patient had more than
9 months from the diagnosis before getting treated, his survival chances are worse. That is a good indication of the
  treatment being effective.

What is very interesting to me from the data is that the patients already survived 9 months as indicated by the disease
duration value but after getting the treatment, there are so many observations of death in the interval from 0 to 100
days. This is quite puzzling and I would deduce intuitively from this information that the treatment is causing the
death. Some more information about how the data was collected would be useful for putting this strange and pessimistic
theory into light of day.
